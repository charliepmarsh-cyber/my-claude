01 — EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

From founder-led hustle to a growth operating system

CortexCart is an AI-powered ecommerce analytics platform that unifies Shopify, WooCommerce, Meta and GA4 data into one dashboard and explains — in plain English — why a store's numbers are moving. The product was strong and differentiated. What surrounded it was typical of an early-stage, founder-led SaaS: growth activity that lived in the founder's head, ad-hoc outreach, and no single view of pipeline, marketing or finances.

CPM Growth Systems was engaged as a strategic growth partner — not to "add AI", but to design and install the commercial and operational infrastructure the product deserved. Over the engagement we built CortexCart OS (an internal dashboard spanning sales, growth, finance and marketing), a repeatable outbound lead generation system with AI qualification and CRM workflow, a marketing asset library including a brand film and campaign kit, and worked directly with the founder on positioning and go-to-market strategy.

The result: growth work that was previously manual and untracked now runs through one shared system, with documented processes and a staged roadmap. The engagement grew into an ongoing partnership.

02 — BUSINESS CONTEXT

A differentiated product at the start of its commercial journey

CortexCart, founded by Jonathan S, gives ecommerce operators the analytics capability of an enterprise data team at a price an independent store owner can justify. It answers the question dashboards usually leave open: why did the numbers move?

Product

One dashboard, plain-English answers

Unifies Shopify, WooCommerce, Meta and GA4 into a single view, with AI explanations of what changed and what to do about it — including revenue arriving from AI assistants like ChatGPT and Perplexity, a channel most analytics tools can't see.

Market

A crowded, expensive category

Incumbent ecommerce analytics tools price for scale-ups, not independents. CortexCart's opportunity: growing store owners who need answers, not another dashboard subscription that costs more than their apps combined.

Stage

Founder-led and early

Strong engineering, real product differentiation, early commercial traction — and a founder personally carrying product, sales and marketing at the same time. The classic early-SaaS constraint: not ideas, capacity.

04 — APPROACH

Operations first. Systems second. AI where it earns its place.

The method is deliberately unglamorous: understand how the business actually works before building anything, prototype fast, and only automate a process once it's been mapped and simplified. AI is applied where judgement is needed — not sprinkled everywhere.

Understand the business, not the tech stack

  • ICP & market review

Map how growth work really happens

Prospect research & Shopify store discovery Stages 1–2+

The system identifies ecommerce stores that fit CortexCart's ideal customer profile and builds a research file on each: platform, apps in use, marketing channels and store context. Existing prospect lists join the same flow through a friendly Excel/CSV import that maps whatever column headers the spreadsheet arrives with.

  • ICP-matched store discovery rather than bulk scraping
  • Every lead carries cited research notes — sources included
  • Excel import so no existing list gets left behind
Buying-signal detection Stage 3+

Deterministic checks detect the signals that historically correlate with a store being ready for analytics: are they spending on paid ads (ad pixels present), do they run a serious email stack, is there an analytics gap the product directly fills, and are they hiring for marketing or CRO roles?

  • Paid-advertising pixel detection — evidence of spend to optimise
  • Email marketing stack — evidence of retention focus
  • Analytics-gap detection — the "why" the product answers
  • Hiring signals — growth intent in the open
AI qualification & lead prioritisation Stages 4–5+

On top of the rule-based score, Claude produces a per-lead fit analysis: how likely this store is to buy, the strongest sales angle to open with, and the objections most likely to come up. Leads land in the pipeline ranked hot, warm or cold — so the founder's selling time goes to the prospects most likely to convert.

  • Likelihood-to-buy assessment per lead
  • Best sales angle, grounded in that store's actual signals
  • Predicted objections, so responses are prepared before the call
Outreach drafts & CRM-integrated sales workflow Stages 6–7+

The draft generator writes first-touch outreach in CortexCart's brand voice, built from the research file — and a human reviews every message before it goes anywhere. From first reply onward the CRM carries the deal through defined stages, with priority tags keeping the follow-up queue honest.

  • Research-grounded drafts, never blind templates
  • Human review as a hard gate before sending
  • Pipeline stages: contacted → meeting → demo → proposal → won/lost

10 — LESSONS LEARNED

What this engagement proved

01

Start with the operation, not the AI

The biggest gains came from mapping and simplifying workflows before automating them. AI earned its place where judgement was needed — qualification, drafting, search — and deterministic rules handled everything else, cheaper and more predictably.

02

Decide with working software

Prototypes reviewed against real prospect data settled in days what slide decks would have debated for weeks. Features that didn't change behaviour were cut early.

03

One shared system beats four good tools

Consolidation was worth more than any individual feature. When founder and partner work from the same live data, alignment stops being a meeting and becomes a property of the system.

04

Founder time is the scarcest input — design for leverage

Every build was judged by one test: does this move the founder from doing the work to directing it? Systems that create capacity compound; systems that create admin don't survive.

05

Ship the smallest system that changes behaviour

A staged roadmap beat a big-bang rebuild. Each stage went live, proved itself in use, and funded confidence in the next — the same discipline now written into the V2 plan.
